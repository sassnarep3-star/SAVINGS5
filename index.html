What the data says

  • The spread between the best and worst verified rate is enormous. CIT Bank pays 3.75% on $250,000 and Wells Fargo pays 0.01% — a difference of about $9,350 of interest in one year. CIT Bank Wells Fargo
  • Several of the largest banks do not publish a consumer-readable savings APY. Chase, Bank of America and Citibank require a ZIP code, TD serves an empty rate table, PNC replaces its page with a geo-restriction notice, Citizens prints unresolved template tokens where its APYs should be, and Fifth Third quotes promotional rates only in a branch. All 7 of those APY cells are left blank rather than estimated. Chase BofA
  • The highest rate is not always the highest first-year return. SoFi's 3.30% base rate plus a 1.10% six-month direct-deposit boost produces a 3.85% year-1 blended yield — about $746 more than the 3.55% flat rate on $250,000, but only if the direct deposit is set up. SoFi terms
  • Fees can erase the entire return at low balances. Wells Fargo Way2Save pays 0.01% — about $25 a year on $250,000 — and charges $5 a month unless the $300 balance waiver is met. On $10,000 the annual fee is 0.6% of the balance, sixty times the interest rate. Wells Fargo fee terms
  • $250,000 is a hard ceiling per bank, per ownership category. Covering $1,000,000 as a single owner requires four separate FDIC-insured banks; a joint account with two owners raises the per-bank ceiling to $500,000. FDIC coverage rules

How much of your deposit is actually insured

FDIC insurance is per depositor, per bank, per ownership category — not per account, and not per dollar. A single-owner savings account stops being insured above $250,000 at any one bank.

Bottom line. For a balance at or below $250,000, a single high-rate online account from a large FDIC-insured bank — CIT Bank at 3.75%, Barclays at 3.65% or Synchrony Bank at 3.55% — produces several times the interest of a legacy branch account at the same institution size. Above $250,000 the constraint stops being rate and becomes structure: the money has to be split across banks.
