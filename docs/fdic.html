FDIC insurance and how to hold the maximum

The goal is the largest fully-insured balance at the best rate. That means understanding two separate levers: ownership categories (more coverage at one bank) and more banks (a fresh $250,000 at each). Everything on this page is sourced to fdic.gov.

Coverage limits by ownership category

Deposits in different ownership categories at the same bank are insured separately. This is the cheapest way to get past $250,000 without opening accounts everywhere.

Check each institution's FDIC certificate number before assuming two brands are two banks. Two brands sharing one charter share one $250,000 limit.

Two traps when spreading money around

  • Brands are not charters. Capital One states that Capital One and Discover Bank deposit accounts opened on or after 18 May 2025 are counted together for FDIC coverage. FDIC BankFind confirms Discover Bank (cert 5649) became inactive on 18 May 2025. Treating them as two banks would leave money uninsured. source↗ BankFind↗
  • "Up to $3M FDIC" is a program, not a limit. SoFi advertises additional FDIC insurance up to $3,000,000 through a network of participating banks. The FDIC's own limit is still $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per category — the extra coverage depends on balances being swept to other insured banks, and on you not already holding deposits at those banks. source↗

FDIC facts used on this site

FactSource
Deposit insurance is automatic for any deposit account opened at an FDIC-insured bank; customers do not buy it.source↗
Coverage protects the balance of each depositor's account dollar-for-dollar up to the limit, including principal and accrued interest through the date the insured bank closes.source↗
Deposits held in different ownership categories at the same bank are separately insured, so one depositor can hold well over $250,000 fully insured at a single bank.source↗
All deposits an accountholder has in the same ownership category at the same bank are added together and insured up to the standard insurance amount.source↗
If you have accounts at different FDIC-insured banks, the limit applies separately at each bank.source↗
Deposit insurance covers checking, savings, money market deposit accounts and CDs. It does not cover stocks, bonds, mutual funds, annuities, life insurance or other non-deposit products, and it does not cover losses from theft or fraud.source↗
Since the FDIC was established in 1933, no depositor has lost one penny of FDIC-insured funds.source↗
