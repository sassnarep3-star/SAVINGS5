Is the rate actually competitive?

Measured against the FDIC's own published figures for 19 Oct 2026: a savings national average of 0.38% and a national rate cap of 4.63%. The cap is the regulatory ceiling a less-than-well-capitalised bank may pay, so it is a fair read on the top of the market.

Competitiveness over the life of the investment

What the comparison says

  • The best verified no-conditions savings APY, 3.75% at CIT Bank, is 9.9× the FDIC national average and sits 0.88% below the national rate cap.
  • Liquid savings at the top of the market (3.75%) currently beats the FDIC's national average for a 12-month CD (1.76%) by 1.99 points — with no term commitment and no early-withdrawal penalty.
  • The standard rates at the biggest branch networks (0.01%–0.05%) are below the national average of 0.38%. On $250,000 over one year the gap between 3.75% and 0.01% is $9,350.00.
  • Because the rate is variable, the right question is not “what is the rate today” but “does this bank keep paying near the top”. Nothing on this page predicts that; re-check the rate each quarter.
