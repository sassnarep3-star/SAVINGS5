1. How banks were selected

The starting universe was the largest active FDIC-insured institutions by total assets, pulled from the FDIC BankFind Suite institutions API rather than from any ranking article. Every institution in the study sits between $56.8 billion and $4.09 trillion in total assets, and 11 of the 16 active institutions are chartered as national banks; the rest are state-chartered banks that still fall inside the same size screen. The intent was to answer the question as asked: big national banks, no small local banks, no credit unions, and no fintech front-ends whose deposits sit at a partner bank.

Deliberately excluded: fintechs that are not banks (their deposits are held at a partner institution, which is where the FDIC coverage actually applies), credit unions (NCUA-insured, a different scheme), and brokerage cash-sweep programs.

2. How rates were captured

Each rate was read from the bank's own page on 2026-10-22 and transcribed with the bank's own effective date. Where a bank publishes tiered rates, every tier is recorded, with the balance band and the condition attached to it. Where a rate is returned by a ZIP-gated tool, an empty table, or a template placeholder, the rate was left blank. No rate was taken from a search snippet, a comparison site, or an older cached page.

3. Return arithmetic

APY is an annualized rate that already accounts for compounding, so:

balance after n years = deposit × (1 + APY/100)n

and interest = balance - deposit. Where a documented promotional rate applies for part of the first year, the year-1 figure uses a blended rate and the derivation is printed: SoFi's six months at 4.40% and six months at 3.30% gives 1.0440.5 × 1.0330.5 - 1 = 3.85%. Marcus's own page states that one referral produces a combined 3.75% APY for the year, and that is the figure used.

The fallback column on the Returns page applies the FDIC national savings rate (0.38%, as of 2026-10-19) after year one. It is a stress case that shows what a rate cut would cost, not a prediction.

4. FDIC coverage arithmetic

Coverage is $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category. A joint account with two owners therefore raises the ceiling at a single bank to $500,000; a trust account uses the FDIC formula owners × beneficiaries × $250,000, capped at $1,250,000 per owner. The ladder plans never place more than $250,000 at one bank, and a plan that would need more distinct banks than the study contains is labelled “not fully covered” instead of being quietly double-counted.

5. What is deliberately not done

  • No estimation of missing rates. 6 of 17 accounts have no APY on this site because the bank did not publish one in a readable form.
  • No blending of bonuses into APY. Bonuses are worth real money but they are one-off; adding them to a rate would misstate the ongoing yield.
  • No security ranking. FDIC status and the existence of an app are verified; security controls are not audited.
  • No tax or inflation adjustment. No current official tax rate or inflation index was fetched for this study, so none is applied.
  • No recommendation of a single bank. Rate, balance, fee waiver and FDIC ceiling interact; the tables let you price your own combination.

6. Reproducing this

The entire site is generated from the JSON files in data/ by scripts in tools/; no number on a page is typed by hand. tools/build_returns.py produces the projections, tools/build_site.py renders the pages, and tools/check_site.py fails the build if any table row lacks a source link or any internal link is broken.
