How competitive are these rates?

Each APY is measured against the FDIC's own national averages and against Treasury yields, so the number can be judged rather than just compared with the other banks in the table.

Every rate against the benchmarks

The FDIC national savings rate on 2026-10-19 was 0.38%. The best verified rate in this study is 3.75% — about 9.9 times the national average. That sounds decisive, and against a branch savings account it is. Against the rest of the market it is less so: a 12-month Treasury yielded 4.54% on the same date.

The honest conclusion on competitiveness. At 3.75%, the best savings account in this study still pays 79 basis points less than a 12-month Treasury and 134 basis points less than a 60-month Treasury on the FDIC's own comparison date. Savings accounts win on liquidity and on FDIC insurance, not on yield. What the table does show is that the gap between the best and worst savings account in this study is about 374 basis points (Wells Fargo pays 0.01%), and the gap between the best account and the FDIC national savings average of 0.38% is about 337 basis points — that is the part you control.

Life-span effect

A savings APY is variable. Every bank in this table reserves the right to change it, and several say so explicitly on the page the rate came from. That makes the 5-year projections on the Returns page a constant-rate arithmetic exercise, not a forecast. The same page shows a fallback scenario where the rate drops to the FDIC national average after year one; the difference between the two columns is the value of the rate staying put, which no bank promises.

CD rates are included in the benchmark table only for context. This study does not recommend CDs, because the required holding period conflicts with the liquidity the account is meant to provide.
