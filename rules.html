Fee drag is the annual fee divided by the balance, as a percentage. It is not subtracted from the APY shown elsewhere; it is shown separately because whether you pay it depends on the waiver.

The fee is the hidden cost in this comparison. A $5 monthly fee is $60 a year. On a $10,000 balance that is 0.60% — more than the entire APY of several accounts in this table. At $100,000 the same fee is 0.06%, which is why the fee matters most to exactly the balances where the headline rate looks least attractive.

Rules worth flagging

  • Ally limits certain withdrawals and transfers to 10 per statement cycle and may close the account if the limit is exceeded more than occasionally. Ally
  • Truist One Savings charges $5 per withdrawal over six in a statement cycle, capped at six fees. Truist disclosure
  • TD Simple Savings has no minimum opening deposit, but TD states the account closes automatically if no deposit is made within 45 days. TD
  • Bank of America Advantage Savings requires $100 to open, does not allow paper checks, and is set to decline all overdrafts. BofA fee schedule
  • SoFi automatically opens a checking account alongside the savings account. SoFi
  • Schwab has no online application for Investor Savings; the application is downloaded, mailed, uploaded through secure messaging or brought to a branch. Schwab
  • Wells Fargo requires 18+ to apply online; 17 and under must open at a branch. Wells Fargo
